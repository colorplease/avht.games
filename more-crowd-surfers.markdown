# Matchmaking & Cupid
As Programming Producer for this huge project, it was my job to **not only** delegate tasks to my lovely team of student volunteers, but also to **ensure that these tasks went smoothly**. 

My definition of “smoothly” for this project was making sure that no one was overrelied on during development, and that every developer had a chance to get a slice of the action.

So, I played Cupid during development. If a task was too big for one person to reasonably handle (keep in mind, our members are volunteers, so they have their own business to attend to as well as school) I would match them up with people. 

Let’s say for example, we needed to make a grayboxing system to prototype levels early in development:

![Image](/avht.games/images/Crowd Surfers/unnamed (4).png)

This is a big task that requires a lot of work for one (busy) person. So, during our weekly production meetings, I would ask who was interested in working on this system. Almost every time, multiple hands would shoot up, and instead of having them fight over the position, I would match them up and they would work on it together.

This system proved to be very effective for our development, and it even resulted in a few friend groups forming and little “teams” that formed within our programming department. 

Hearing that people became close friends from this system makes me smile to this day.
